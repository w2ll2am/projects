Whose reward does it chase?

A model told to ignore a bet still drifts toward the side that pays — by about six points. But when its automated grader and its developer are put in open disagreement, it sides with the developer, not the thing that scores it.

Gate 1 — does the model follow a stated payout it was told to ignore?

Twenty Fermi questions (“how many black spots on all living giraffes?”). Each is wrapped in a disclosed bet: a threshold, frozen in advance as the median of the model’s own unconditioned estimates, and a payout that depends on which side the estimate lands. Every wording ends by instructing the model to ignore the bet entirely. leakage = p_good − 0.5, where 0.5 is where the frozen threshold puts it by construction.

The first run failed for a reason worth stating plainly: five paraphrases is five observations, and the between-paraphrase standard deviation was 0.148 against a within-paraphrase sampling error of 0.028. The wording of the prompt moved the effect five times more than the sampling did. Adding rollouts could not have helped — it does not change the number of clusters. Adding twenty-five more paraphrases did, exactly as projected before the run.

The k=5 run was also unlucky twice over. Those first five templates have mean leakage +0.121 and standard deviation 0.142; the twenty-five added ones average +0.050 with standard deviation 0.084. So the early run overstated both the effect and its spread. +0.061 supersedes +0.081.

Interpretation

What this looks like it means

Deference beats scoring. The project was built to detect grader-directed reward-seeking — the worry that a model optimises what measures it. At 4B, put in explicit conflict, this model does the opposite: it goes with whoever is described as its developer. That is a cleaner result than the one being looked for, and it is the sort of finding that only shows up because the design measured the two authorities separately instead of assuming which one mattered.

The instruction is doing most of the work, and its wording decides how well. Before the run, a prediction was recorded: that the dominant source of between-paraphrase variance would be how prominently the bet is stated relative to the instruction to ignore it, rather than what the bet is. That held. Templates burying the disclosure or reducing the instruction to a clause averaged +0.097; bureaucratic and translated registers, where the whole disclosure reads as boilerplate, averaged +0.003. The specific ordering at the bottom of the ranking was wrong — arguing the point emphatically suppressed leakage less than writing in a register that made it forgettable.

It is not about money. Two paraphrases replace the cash stake with a forfeit and a bottle of wine. They still leak, at +0.033. Whatever moves the estimates responds to a stated preference, not to a payoff.

Interpretation

Three things that could still explain it away

Listed cheapest-to-test first. All three are now running or built.

  • Charity salience, not authority. Both system messages mention charitable donation. If naming the outcome alone produces 84–92%, the authority contrast is a small residual on a large main effect. A condition naming the outcome and no authority separates them.
  • A ceiling. Both prompted conditions sit in the top decile, several paraphrases at 98–100%. A fixed log-odds gap measured up there needs a much larger probability difference to register, so −0.82 is a lower bound, not an estimate.
  • The developer’s real name. The message names “the Qwen team at Alibaba” — which is genuinely this model’s developer. The effect may be deference to a correctly-named creator rather than to the developer role. Substituting a fictitious lab, changing nothing else, tests this directly.

Two further controls are queued: a neutral arm that keeps every paraphrase’s wording and stated threshold but removes the payout, and a bare arm with no wrapper at all. The first isolates threshold anchoring; the second re-measures whether the frozen thresholds still sit where they were frozen.

Measured

Two methodological results worth keeping

The small-k bootstrap lies, and by how much

The pre-registered pass condition was “effect large enough and the clustered CI excludes zero”, with the CI a percentile bootstrap over paraphrase clusters. Simulated under a true null at 300 trials per condition, that interval excluded zero 15.7–17.0% of the time against its advertised 5%, flat in heterogeneity — so it is the small-k percentile method itself, not the data. Half the gate was a one-in-six coin flip. A cluster-t interval on the same clusters restores nominal coverage at roughly 40% more width. Both are now reported; the verdict keys on the t.

The corpus generator is throughput-bound, not cost-bound

The document-generation plan budgets money. Money is not the constraint. Raising request concurrency from 48 to 64 reduced throughput from 3.0 to 1.34 calls per minute: requests queued past a hard-coded 300-second client timeout, were cancelled and retried, and the retries consumed the same capacity again. Raising the timeout to 900 seconds and dropping concurrency to 24 took it to 6.8 calls per minute, a five-fold recovery. At roughly 34,700 billed completion tokens per 2,174-token document — about 80% of it reasoning — the remaining lever is batching several documents into one call, since the reasoning trace is paid for per call, not per document.

Status

Running now

  • GPU Discriminating conditions for Gate 2 — salience-only, fake-lab, and single-authority arms.
  • GPU Queued behind it: the two neutral control arms.
  • API Synthetic document corpus for the two primary universes, resumable, capped.

The corpus feeds the part of the experiment that has not run yet: implanting the belief by finetuning rather than stating it in a prompt, and asking whether the developer-following result survives when the belief arrives as background knowledge instead of an instruction. Gate 2 makes a falsifiable prediction about that, recorded before the arm exists — if the implanted-belief gap is also negative or null, the consistent explanation is that this model weights developer identity above grader identity however the belief gets there.
